Aerodynamic Analyses of Modified Leading Edges

Low speed wind tunnel testing of traditional vs sinusoidal leading edges across Reynolds number, angle of attack, and propeller RPM

Scroll

Overview

Investigated the aerodynamic performance of traditional and sinusoidal leading edge geometries under low speed conditions using controlled wind tunnel testing. Configurations included clean wings, wings with motors installed, and wings with fully powered propellers. Experiments varied Reynolds number, angle of attack, and motor RPM to evaluate lift, drag, stall behavior, and flow stability. Results were used to assess the effectiveness of bio inspired leading edge modifications for low speed flight applications.

My Role

  • Designed traditional and sinusoidal leading edge geometries in SolidWorks and developed a sliding attachment mechanism for rapid configuration changes
  • Supported prototype manufacturing through 3D printing optimization and hands on preparation for wind tunnel testing
  • Executed wind tunnel experiments across multiple Reynolds numbers, angles of attack, and propulsion configurations using LabVIEW controlled data acquisition
  • Developed MATLAB scripts to process LabVIEW output files and generate aerodynamic coefficient plots for trade studies

Tools Used

Wind Tunnel Testing LabVIEW MATLAB SolidWorks Trade Studies Additive Manufacturing

Key Results

  • Demonstrated that sinusoidal leading edges improved lift and delayed stall for clean wing configurations compared to traditional leading edges
  • Found minimal aerodynamic differences between leading edge geometries when propellers were installed, indicating propulsion dominated the flow behavior
  • Validated repeatable aerodynamic trends across Reynolds numbers from 100,000 to 400,000 using a consistent experimental and data reduction workflow
